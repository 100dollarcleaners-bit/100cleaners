# Complete Hiring Guide for Cleaning Companies

**The Cleaning CEO Blueprint — 100% Cleaner Academy**

This guide walks you through hiring your first cleaner — and your tenth — without destroying your margins or your reputation.

---

## When to Hire

### Hire your first cleaner when:

- You are **turning away bookings** or working 50+ hours/week yourself
- You have **recurring revenue** covering at least 60% of a full week's payroll
- Your SOPs, checklists, and supplies are **documented** (you can't train without them)
- You can **afford 2–4 weeks** of payroll before the new hire is fully productive

### Do NOT hire when:

- You're hiring out of panic after one busy week
- You don't know your true profit per job
- You have no quality control system (photos, inspections, callbacks)
- Cash flow can't cover a slow week + a no-show

### Hiring Timeline Benchmark

| Stage | Your Business |
|-------|---------------|

| First 1–2 cleaners | $8K–$20K/month |

| Multi-team operation | $50K+/month |

## Independent Contractor vs. Employee

**This is not legal advice. Consult a local employment attorney or CPA before classifying workers.**

### Independent Contractor (1099) — Common in cleaning startups

**Typical traits (varies by state):**
- Controls their own schedule within agreed windows
- Uses own vehicle (sometimes)
- Paid per job, not hourly
- Can work for other companies
- Responsible for their own taxes

**Pros:** Lower admin burden, per-job pay aligns with revenue  
**Cons:** Misclassification risk if you micromanage like an employee; state laws differ sharply (especially CA, NY, NJ)

### W-2 Employee

**Typical traits:**
- You set schedule, assign jobs, provide supplies
- You withhold taxes, may owe workers' comp and unemployment
- More control over quality and brand

**Pros:** Legal clarity when you direct daily work; easier to enforce uniforms and SOPs  
**Cons:** Higher cost (payroll taxes, insurance, benefits); more paperwork

### The 100% Cleaner Recommendation

Start with the classification your **state law and accountant** recommend — not what Facebook groups tell you. Many owners begin with per-job 1099 arrangements and transition to W-2 as they scale and exert more operational control.

**Document everything:** agreements, pay rates, scope of work, termination terms.

## Full Hiring Process (Step by Step)

### Phase 1: Prepare (Before You Post)

- [ ] Finalize pay rates (see Pay Structure below)
- [ ] Write job post with clear expectations
- [ ] Set up interview scoring sheet ([Interview Questions](interview-questions.md))
- [ ] Choose background check provider
- [ ] Prepare trial clean job (paid, real home or your own)
- [ ] Create onboarding packet (SOPs, checklists, contact list)

### Phase 2: Recruit (Week 1)

**Where to post:**
- Indeed, Craigslist, Facebook local groups
- Nextdoor (service pros section)
- Referrals from existing cleaners (offer referral bonus)
- Instagram/Facebook story: "Hiring cleaners — flexible schedule"

**Job post must include:**
- Pay range (per job or hourly — be specific)
- Service area / cities covered
- Requirements: transportation, background check, smartphone
- Part-time vs. full-time availability
- Link or email to apply

### Phase 3: Screen (Week 1–2)

1. **Application review** — filter for experience, location, availability
2. **15-minute phone screen** — use 5 questions from interview guide
3. **Schedule formal interview** — only top candidates

### Phase 4: Interview (Week 2)

- 30–45 minute in-person or video interview
- Score every answer using rubric
- Explain pay structure, expectations, trial clean
- Minimum score: 70/100 to advance

### Phase 5: Background Check

Run **after** strong interview, **before** or **during** trial clean.

**Recommended checks:**
- Criminal background (county + national)
- Sex offender registry (if allowed in your state)
- Motor vehicle record (if they drive to jobs)
- Identity verification

**Popular providers:** Checkr, GoodHire, Sterling, Accurate Background

**Cost:** ~$30–$80 per candidate. Build into hiring budget.

**Note:** Follow FCRA guidelines — get written consent, provide adverse action notices if you decline based on report.

### Phase 6: Paid Trial Clean (Week 2–3)

- Pay full job rate ($100 regular / $200 deep or your rates)
- Assign experienced cleaner or you shadow
- Use trial scorecard from interview guide
- Client home (with permission) or your home staged as client

**Pass criteria:** Quality 4+/5, on time, followed SOP, professional communication

### Phase 7: Offer & Onboarding (Week 3)

See Onboarding section below.

## Pay Structure

### The 100% Cleaner Model (Per Job)

| Service | Customer Pays | Cleaner Pay | Company Profit | Supply Est. |
|---------|--------------|-------------|----------------|-------------|
| Regular Clean | $150 | $100 | $50 | ~$8–$12 |
| Deep Clean | $300 | $200 | $100 | ~$15–$25 |

**Cleaner receives ~67% of job revenue.** Your 33% covers:
- Scheduling and admin
- Marketing and lead cost
- Insurance and taxes
- Supplies (if you provide)
- Callbacks and quality issues
- Your salary as owner

### Pay Schedule

| Model | Frequency | Best For |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| Weekly | Every Friday | Cash flow stability for cleaners |
| Bi-weekly | Every other Friday | Easier admin |
| Per job (same day) | After completion | 1099 contractors, small volume |

**Always pay on time.** Late pay is the #1 reason cleaners leave or go independent.

### Raises & Bonuses

- **After 90 days:** $5–$10/job increase if quality scores 4.5+ average
- **Referral bonus:** $100–$250 per hired cleaner who stays 90 days
- **Perfect month bonus:** No call-offs, 4.8+ client ratings
- **Lead cleaner premium:** +$10–$15/job for training new hires on-site

## Background Checks — Deep Dive

### What to look for

- **Theft, burglary, fraud** — high concern for in-home access
- **Violent offenses** — case-by-case with legal counsel
- **Recent DUI/reckless driving** — if driving company or client property

### What requires nuance

- Old minor offenses (years ago, non-violent)
- Marijuana convictions in legal states
- Eviction history (not always on criminal report)

**Have a written hiring policy** so decisions are consistent, not arbitrary.

### Bonding (Optional)

Janitorial bonding insures clients against theft. Costs ~$100–$500/year. Market as "bonded and insured" once active.

## Onboarding Checklist

### Day 1 — Paperwork & Orientation (2–3 hours)

- [ ] Signed contractor agreement or W-4 + I-9
- [ ] Direct deposit / payment info
- [ ] Emergency contact
- [ ] Background check consent (if not done)
- [ ] Review company values, dress code, communication rules
- [ ] Issue supplies kit or confirm they have required items
- [ ] Add to scheduling app, group chat, GPS tracking
- [ ] Walk through SOPs folder (Module 5 downloads)

### Day 2–5 — Shadow Days

- [ ] Shadow you or lead cleaner on 2–3 jobs
- [ ] Perform tasks with supervision
- [ ] Practice photo documentation (arrival, before/after, issues)
- [ ] Review quality control checklist

### Week 2 — Solo with Backup

- [ ] Assign solo jobs (regular cleans only, no deep/move-out yet)
- [ ] You or lead cleaner available by phone
- [ ] End-of-day debrief first week

### Day 30 — Review

- [ ] Quality audit on 2 random jobs
- [ ] Client feedback review
- [ ] Confirm fit or part ways (document performance)

## Training Priorities (First 30 Days)

1. **SOP compliance** — same result every home
2. **Photo documentation** — protects you and them
3. **Communication** — running late, issues, supply needs
4. **Time management** — regular clean in 2–3 hours for standard 3/2
5. **Client boundaries** — no personal conversations about pricing, no side deals

---

## Retention — Keep Good Cleaners

- Pay on time, every time
- Don't overload schedule (burnout kills quality)
- Respond to texts same day
- Celebrate wins publicly in team chat
- Clear path to lead cleaner role
- Fair callback policy (not always cleaner's fault)
- Listen when they say a client is unreasonable

---

## Termination — When & How

### Terminate when:

- Repeated no-shows without notice
- Theft or dishonesty (document everything)
- Client safety concerns
- Refusal to follow SOPs after written warning
- Background issue discovered that violates policy

### Process:

1. Verbal warning (document date and issue)
2. Written warning with specific improvement plan (7–14 days)
3. Final written warning
4. Termination letter — pay all owed wages immediately per state law
5. Recover keys, supplies, company shirt
6. Remove from apps, group chats, client access
