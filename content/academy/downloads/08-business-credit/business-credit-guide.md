# Business Credit Building Guide

**The Cleaning CEO Blueprint — 100% Cleaner Academy**

Business credit helps you access vendor terms, better insurance rates, and financing — **when built responsibly**. This guide teaches smart fundamentals. It does **not** promise approvals, specific credit scores, or overnight results.

---

## Important Disclaimer

- **No credit strategy guarantees approval.** Lenders and vendors make independent decisions.
- **This is educational content, not financial or legal advice.** Consult a CPA and business attorney for your situation.
- **"Credit repair" schemes that promise tradelines or CPNs are often illegal.** Avoid them.
- Results depend on revenue, time in business, payment history, and industry risk profile.

---

## Why Business Credit Matters for Cleaners

| Benefit | Real-World Use |
|---------|----------------|
| Vendor net-30 terms | Buy supplies now, pay in 30 days — smooths cash flow |
| Business credit cards | Separate expenses, earn rewards, float payroll gap |
| Equipment financing | Vacuums, vehicles, extractors without draining cash |
| Insurance premiums | Some carriers review business financial health |
| Professional credibility | Landlords and commercial clients may request DUNS or credit profile |

**You do not need business credit to start.** Many successful cleaning companies run on personal savings and revenue for the first year. Build credit when cash flow is stable — not as day-one priority.

## Step 1: Separate Business and Personal Finances

**Non-negotiable foundation.**

### Checklist

- [ ] LLC or corporation formed (see [LLC Checklist](../01-getting-started/llc-checklist.md))
- [ ] EIN from IRS (free at irs.gov)
- [ ] Business checking account (never commingle personal funds)
- [ ] Business debit/credit card for all company expenses
- [ ] Bookkeeping system (QuickBooks, Wave, or spreadsheet)
- [ ] Business address (registered agent OK; some vendors want physical address)

### Why Separation Matters

- Personal liability protection weakens when you mix funds
- Business credit bureaus track **business** accounts — not your personal Netflix charges
- Clean books = easier loans and tax filing

---

## Step 2: Register Your Business Profile

### Dun & Bradstreet (D-U-N-S Number)

- Free to obtain at dnb.com
- Many vendors and government contracts reference DUNS
- Update company info annually

### Business Credit Bureaus

| Bureau | What They Track |
|--------|-----------------|
| Dun & Bradstreet | Trade lines, PAYDEX score |
| Experian Business | Credit accounts, public records |
| Equifax Business | Vendor reports, liens |

You don't need perfect scores on day one. You need **consistent, verifiable business identity.**

---

## Step 3: Vendor Accounts (Starter Trade Lines)

Vendor accounts are supplier relationships that report payment history to business credit bureaus.

### Starter Vendors (Commonly Used — Approval Not Guaranteed)

| Vendor | What They Sell | Typical Terms |
|--------|----------------|---------------|
| Uline | Shipping, supplies, paper goods | Net 30 |
| Quill | Office supplies | Net 30 |
| Grainger | Industrial/cleaning equipment | Net 30 |
| Summa | Office furniture/supplies | Net 30 |
| Strategic | Business supplies | Net 30 |

### How to Approach Vendor Credit

1. Order small amounts first ($50–$100)
2. Pay **early** or on time — every time
3. Ask if they report to D&B or Experian Business
4. Build 3–5 vendor relationships over 6–12 months
5. Don't open accounts you don't need just for credit

---

## Step 4: Business Credit Cards

### When You're Ready

- 6+ months in business
- Consistent revenue ($3K+/month minimum)
- Clean personal credit helps (many issuers require personal guarantee for small business)

### Responsible Use Rules

| Do | Don't |
|----|-------|
| Pay balance in full monthly | Carry balance at 20%+ APR |
| Use for supplies, fuel, ads | Use for personal expenses |
| Keep utilization under 30% | Max out cards before payroll |
| Set up autopay | Miss payments |

### Starter Card Types

- **Cash-back business card** — simple, rewards on supplies
- **Secured business card** — if limited history (deposit = credit limit)
- **Store cards** — Lowe's, Home Depot for equipment (use sparingly)

## Step 5: Build PAYDEX and Trade History

**PAYDEX** (D&B) measures how early or late you pay vendors (0–100).

| PAYDEX | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| 80+ | Paid on time or early |
| 70–79 | Occasionally late |
| Below 70 | Frequently late — hurts future approvals |

### Timeline (Realistic)

| Month | Milestone |
|-------|-----------|
| 1–3 | EIN, bank account, first vendor accounts |
| 4–6 | 2–3 vendor lines reporting, first business card |
| 7–12 | PAYDEX establishing, higher limits |
| 12–24 | Equipment financing, line of credit possible |

**There is no 30-day shortcut.** Anyone selling "80 PAYDEX in 30 days" is overselling.

## Debt Traps to Avoid

### 1. Credit Stacking

Opening multiple loans or LOC back-to-back to pull cash. High interest, personal guarantees, and debt spiral risk.

### 2. Revenue-Based "Advances"

Merchant cash advances (MCA) for cleaning companies — factor rates of 1.3–1.5 mean you repay $13K–$15K on a $10K advance. Use only in true emergencies with a repayment plan.

### 3. Personal Guarantee Everything

Most small business credit requires your personal guarantee early on. That's normal. **Abnormal:** taking on debt your revenue can't service in 90 days.

### 4. Buying Tradelines

Paying to "rent" someone else's credit history — often fraudulent and can destroy your business reputation.

### 5. Neglecting Taxes for Credit

A business credit card doesn't replace IRS obligations. Tax liens destroy business credit overnight.

## Financing Options as You Scale

| Option | Best For | Caution |
|--------|----------|---------|
| SBA microloan | Equipment, working capital | Paperwork, slow process |
| Business line of credit | Seasonal cash gaps | Only if you have repayment discipline |
| Equipment lease | Vacuums, vehicles | Total cost higher than buying |
| Invoice factoring | Commercial contracts | Expensive; rare for residential |
| Revenue-based financing | Fast cash | High effective APR |

**Rule:** If you can't explain how you'll repay it from **existing or contracted revenue**, don't borrow.

---

## What Lenders Actually Look At

1. **Time in business** — 2+ years preferred for traditional loans
2. **Revenue** — tax returns and bank statements
3. **Debt service coverage** — can you pay this loan + existing debt?
4. **Industry** — cleaning is service-based, asset-light (higher perceived risk)
5. **Personal credit** — still matters for small business
6. **Business credit file** — trade lines, PAYDEX, no liens

## Monthly Credit Health Checklist

- [ ] All vendor invoices paid on time
- [ ] Credit card balance paid in full
- [ ] No new accounts opened unnecessarily
- [ ] Business credit report reviewed (annual free check where available)
- [ ] D&B profile updated and accurate
- [ ] No personal expenses on business accounts

---

## When to Pause Credit Building

Stop applying for new credit if:

- Revenue dropped 20%+ month over month
- You're using credit to make payroll
- You have unresolved tax issues
- You don't have 3 months operating expenses saved

**Fix cash flow first.** Credit amplifies good operations — it doesn't fix broken ones.

---

## 100% Cleaner Credit Philosophy

1. **Revenue before credit** — sell jobs, then borrow for growth
2. **Separate always** — business accounts only for business
3. **Pay early** — vendor relationships matter in this industry
4. **No guarantees** — every approval is a maybe until it's yes
5. **Avoid debt traps** — MCAs and credit stacking kill more cleaning companies than competition
